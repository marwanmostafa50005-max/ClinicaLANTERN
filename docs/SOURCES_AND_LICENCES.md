# Sources and licences

**Read this first.** This file is a reading of the licence lines that the publisher prints for eight published sources. An AI assistant drafted it for the founder. The founder must confirm each line himself. It is not legal advice. It is not legal clearance.

Until the founder confirms a source, every criterion file that uses it keeps `licence_status: "proposed"`. That label means the project has recorded a licence from a publisher page, from an earlier project extract, or from a delegated reader. It is not the publisher's written confirmation.

Pages read: 18 November 2026. Sources 6 and 7 were read the same day by a page-reading helper of the coordinator, not line by line by a person; section 2 says how. Publishers can change their pages. Check again before any public release.

On 21 November 2026, `criteria\active.json` lists 583 criteria in use. The coordinator's record (`leader-points\sources-of-criteria.md`) lists 63 distinct combinations of source document, licence and licence status. The table below is the dataset assumption for this entry.

## 1. Short answer

- The eight journal sources read in November 2026 (numbered 1 to 8 in the sections below) are open access under Creative Commons Attribution, except the PINCER protocol, which is under Creative Commons Attribution 2.0 Generic (CC BY 2.0).
- Neither CC BY licence used in those readings has a "non-commercial" term or a "no changes" term. Both ask for credit. Section 6 states those conditions for CC BY only.
- Many other sources in the table below were not read line by line for this file. Each keeps `licence_status: proposed` until the founder confirms it.
- Four things are not settled by the pages for sources 1 to 8. Section 8 lists these and some smaller points.
  1. The STOPP criteria text is in a supplementary file. The page prints no licence line for that file.
  2. The publisher's web page for PROMPT does not print the licence sentence. The publisher's PDF and the PubMed Central copy do.
  3. Most supplementary files were not opened.
  4. Sources 6 and 7 were read by a page-reading helper, not line by line by a person. The founder should read the two pages himself before he confirms them.

The page data gives the copyright holder as "The Author(s)", 2023. The criteria are not printed in the article. The article sends the reader to Appendix 1 for the criteria and to Appendix 2 for the references. Both are supplementary files linked from the page. The page prints one licence statement, and it speaks of "this article". It prints no terms for the supplementary files. The text of Appendix 1 was read. The reading here is that Appendix 1 is published as part of a CC BY 4.0 article and is most likely under the same licence. The page does not say so in words. ## 6. What the two licences ask for

This is a plain summary of the licence text. The licence text itself decides.

**CC BY 4.0** (Section 3(a) of the licence). If you share the material, changed or not, you must:

- keep the names of the creators, in the way they ask;
- keep the copyright notice;
- keep a notice that refers to the licence;
- keep a notice that refers to the disclaimer of warranties;
- keep a link to the material, where that is reasonably practicable;
- say if you changed the material;
- say that the material is under CC BY 4.0, and give the licence text or a link to it.

The first five points apply when the licensor supplied that item with the material. The licence says the points may be met "in any reasonable manner" for the medium. It gives no right to suggest that the authors endorse your use. It does not license patents or trade marks.

**CC BY 2.0** (Section 4 of the licence). You must:

- give the licence address with every copy;
- keep all copyright notices;
- give the name of the original author and the title of the work;
- give the address that the licensor names for the work, where that is reasonably practicable;
- for an adapted work, say how the original was used, for example "Adapted from ...";
- keep the notices that refer to the licence and to the disclaimer of warranties.

So each credit needs five things: title, authors, source, licence, and what was changed.
